N°02 — Anatomy of an Oyster

Five chambers of precision.

SECTION A–ASCALE 8:1
01Oystersteel Case
01 / CASE

The Oyster case

A hermetically sealed monobloc middle case machined from a block of 904L Oystersteel — an alloy normally reserved for aerospace and chemistry. Screwed down, it shrugs off pressure, dust and decades.

  • 904L STEEL
  • SCREW-DOWN
  • MONOBLOC
02 / BEZEL

The fluted bezel

Once a functional seal screwed onto the case, the fluting is now cut in precious metal with a precision of a few microns — 96 facets catching light the way Geneva catches snow.

  • 96 FACETS
  • 18 CT GOLD
  • MICRON TOLERANCE
03 / DATE

The Cyclops lens

A sapphire lens bonded over the date window magnifies the numeral two-and-a-half times. Invented by Rolex in the early 1950s, it turned a complication into a silhouette.

  • 2.5× MAGNIFICATION
  • SAPPHIRE
  • ANTI-REFLECTIVE
04 / CROWN

The Triplock crown

Three sealed zones, three gaskets, one gesture. The winding crown screws into the case like a submarine hatch, completing a waterproof system guaranteed to 300 metres.

  • TRIPLE GASKET
  • 300 M
  • SCREW-LOCK
05 / MOVEMENT

The Perpetual rotor

Behind the case back, a free-swinging rotor harvests every motion of the wrist and banks it in the mainspring — seventy hours of autonomy, accurate to −2/+2 seconds a day.

  • 70 H RESERVE
  • 31 JEWELS
  • PARACHROM HAIRSPRING

N°04 — Heritage

One hundred twenty-one years, eleven turning points.

1905

The Vision

Twenty-four-year-old Hans Wilsdorf founds the company in London, convinced the wristwatch can be as precise as any chronometer.

1910

First Certificate

A wristwatch receives the world's first official chronometer rating, granted by the School of Horology in Bienne.

1926

The Oyster

The world's first waterproof wristwatch is born — a hermetically sealed case of bezel, back and crown screwed onto the middle case.

1927

The Channel

Mercedes Gleitze swims the English Channel with an Oyster around her neck. Ten hours later, it keeps perfect time.

1931

Perpetual

The first self-winding mechanism with a free rotor is invented — the Perpetual movement that still winds every Oyster today.

1953

Submariner & Everest

The Submariner dives to 100 m, while an Oyster Perpetual accompanies the expedition that first summits Everest.

1956

Day-Date

The first wristwatch to spell out the day in full alongside the date. It would come to be worn by presidents.

1963

Cosmograph Daytona

Born for the endurance circuits of Florida, built to be the ultimate instrument of speed and the podium.

2022

Deepsea Challenge

Engineered to accompany dives to 11,000 metres — the full depth of the Mariana Trench, in grade-5 titanium.
